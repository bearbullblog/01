The Fall of Silver

30 October 2026

In January 2026, silver hit a record above $121 an ounce. By 30 October, it was about $51. That is a fall of more than half. This post explains what happened.

The short version

  • Record high: $121.64 per ounce on 29 January.
  • Price on 30 October: about $51.3, the lowest since November 2025.
  • Fall from the record: about 56%.
  • Main reasons: rate hikes, a stronger dollar, higher bond yields and higher trading margins.

MCX is India’s commodity exchange. Its price is for 1 kg of silver, while the international price is for 1 ounce.

A one-day crash

On 30 January, silver fell about 31% in a single day. Even so, January ended 19% higher. The real damage came later: silver fell about 8% in September and about 15% in October. October was its second monthly fall in a row.

Even after all this, silver is still about 5% higher than it was a year ago.

Why silver kept falling

1. Higher interest rates. The US Federal Reserve raised its rate to 3.75%–4.00% on 16 September. On 28 October it held the rate, but two of the 12 members voted for another hike. Silver dropped 2.6% to $52.39 on the news. Markets now see an 83.3% chance of another hike in December.

Why does this hurt silver? Silver pays no interest. When banks and bonds pay more, holding silver becomes less attractive.

2. Inflation is still high. US inflation was 3.7% in September. Jobs grew by only 29,000. That makes it hard for the Fed to cut rates soon.

3. Strong dollar and high bond yields. The US 10-year bond yield is near 5%. The dollar index is up about 4% this year. Silver is priced in dollars, so a stronger dollar makes it more expensive for buyers in other countries.

4. Higher margins. A margin is the money a trader must keep aside to hold a futures position. After the January fall, exchanges asked for more:

  • CME raised COMEX silver margins three times. The last rise was 15%, taking the requirement to 18% from 6 February (it was 6% earlier).
  • MCX added an extra 7% margin on 5 and 6 February and removed it on 19 February.
  • Chinese banks stopped retail leveraged gold and silver trading in September.

Higher margins mean less borrowed money in the market. That means fewer buyers.

The gold-silver ratio

This ratio tells you how many ounces of silver you need to buy 1 ounce of gold. It was 55.7 on 6 January and about 77 on 29 October, the highest in 11 months. A higher ratio means silver has done worse than gold.

What to remember

  • Silver is more volatile than gold. It can rise and fall very fast.
  • Interest rates, the dollar and bond yields matter a lot for silver.
  • Trading with borrowed money makes falls bigger. Margin changes can force traders to sell.
  • A big fall does not erase a bigger earlier rise. Silver is still 5% above last year.
