JIO: What’s So Special?

24 October 2026 · 4 min read

It’s 24th October, and I can’t think of a better first blog topic than the mother of all IPOs in India. Jio is gearing up for the country’s biggest public issue ever, and it will be the 4th most valuable listed company from day one. That is wild.

The numbers are huge

Jio is raising ₹30,213 Cr through a fresh issue. The previous record holder, Hyundai, raised ₹27,870 Cr.

What makes this IPO special is that it raises a lot while selling only a tiny slice of the company. Under SEBI’s earlier rule, a company worth more than ₹5 lakh Cr had to offer at least 5% to the public. On 13th March 2026, SEBI changed that: companies above ₹5 lakh Cr can now offer just 2.5%.

Was the rule change made with Jio in mind? Who knows. As we say, “Dosti bani rehni chahiye, baaki jo bhi ho, dekha jayega.” 😄

Where does the money go?

This is a fresh issue, not an OFS (Offer for Sale), which means the money goes into the company, not to existing shareholders. The plan:

  • ₹27,500 Cr for loan repayment
  • ₹2,713 Cr for general corporate purposes

The result is that Jio becomes net debt free.

How Jio was born

2005: The Ambani brothers split the empire. Mukesh took oil, gas, petrochemicals, textiles and refining. Anil took telecom, power, financial services and infrastructure. Mukesh had actually launched Reliance Infocomm in 2002, but it went to Anil, with a 10-year non-compete clause attached.

2010: The non-compete clause ended, and the brothers cancelled their agreements on 23rd May. In June, Infotel Broadband (owned by the Nahata family) won the 4G spectrum auction for ₹12,848 Cr. Within hours, Reliance bought 95% of it and renamed it Reliance Jio Infocomm Ltd. Mukesh was back in telecom.

The licence problem: Infotel only had an internet licence. As per Business Today, calls brought in nearly 85% of India’s telecom revenue in 2013, so internet alone meant fighting over the remaining 15%. That year, Jio paid another ₹1,658 Cr for a unified licence that allowed voice calls.

1st September 2016: the day everything changed

“Customers should pay for only one service, either voice or data. No Jio customer will ever pay for voice calls to any network in India.” Mukesh Ambani

The market average price was about ₹228 per GB. Jio brought it down to ₹50. The competition felt it immediately: Aircel fell 8.99%, Idea fell 9.09% and Reliance Communication fell 5%.

The customer growth was just as dramatic:

  • 50 million customers in 83 days. Airtel took 12 years and Idea took 13 years to get there.
  • 100 million customers in 170 days, which is nearly 7 new customers every second.

Why fresh issue and not OFS?

Jio dropped the planned OFS and went with a fresh issue, reportedly to avoid listing-day losses.

The business model, simply

Telecom revenue is customers × ARPU (average revenue per user). Jio has 524 million customers, and 77% of FY26 revenue comes from prepaid recharges, a steady monthly stream that works a bit like a SIP.

Jio also benefits from operating leverage. Networks are expensive to build, but once they’re up, costs barely move. Revenue grew 14.6% while network costs rose just 3.4%.

Jio sells four things: mobile broadband, fixed broadband, enterprise services and digital services.

So, is it cheap or expensive?

The price band of ₹1,065 to ₹1,119 values Jio at ₹9.81 to 10.30 lakh Cr. The picture is mixed:

  • P/E: Jio is 18 to 22% cheaper than Airtel.
  • Returns: Jio’s RoNW is lower, at 9.42% vs Airtel’s 20.32%.
  • EV/EBITDA: Jio looks expensive.

No single multiple settles it. The real question is this: does Jio deserve a telecom multiple or a tech one?
